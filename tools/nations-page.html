Who runs the UK? Nation by nation

The United Kingdom is made up of four nations: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Each one has organisations that make laws, run public services and settle disagreements. Pick a nation to see them all, what each one does and how the people in charge get there.

New here? Start with the three parts of the state

Law-makers

People we elect to a parliament or assembly. They make laws, decide how money is spent and question the government. (The legislature.)

Government and public services

Ministers, councils and hundreds of organisations that carry out the laws: hospitals, schools, transport, police and more. (The executive.)

Courts and judges

Judges decide whether laws have been broken and settle disagreements fairly. They are chosen independently, not elected. (The judiciary.)

What do these words mean?
Nation
One of the four parts of the UK: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
Public body
An organisation that does a job for the public and is paid for or controlled by government.
Devolved
Powers that the UK Parliament has handed to Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland, such as health and education.
Reserved
Powers the UK Parliament keeps for the whole UK, such as defence and foreign affairs.
Minister
An elected politician chosen to run part of the government.
Elected
Chosen by voters at an election.
Appointed
Chosen by someone in charge (for example a minister or a commission), not by voters.
Council
Local government for a town, city or county, run by councillors elected by local people.
Agency
Part of a government department that runs one service, such as issuing passports.
Arm's-length body
An organisation set up by government to work at a distance from ministers, so it can act independently. Sometimes called a quango.
Regulator
A body that sets rules for an industry or service and checks they are followed.
Whole UK
A body that works in all four nations.
Extent not stated
A UK government body where no source says which nations it covers.

How we decide which nation a body belongs to

We only use trusted sources, in this order: the parliament the body answers to; who funds and oversees it, as the Office for National Statistics records; what the body says about itself on GOV.UK; the nation its official register gives; official local council codes; the law that set it up; Wikidata; a 2020 list of public bodies; and where it is registered as a company. We never guess from a name. What each body does is worked out from its name and description, so it can be wrong: please tell us if it is.
